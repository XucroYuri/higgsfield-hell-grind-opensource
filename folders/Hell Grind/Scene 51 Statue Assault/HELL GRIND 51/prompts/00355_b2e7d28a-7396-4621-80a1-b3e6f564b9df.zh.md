Style: 8K IMAX. Photorealistic — no 3D render, no game engine, no game-cutscene aesthetic.

Lighting: Natural light only — contre-jour backlight, camera on shadow side, atmospheric haze throughout. Key light from sky and windows only.

Camera: Physical cine lens. 180-degree shutter motion blur.

Acting: Hollywood — micro-pauses before reactions, precise eye-line, wet living eyes with catch-lights, visible breath and chest rise.
Physics: Gravity and inertia respected — mass has real weight, correct contact shadows. No floating props.
Composition: Rule of thirds + golden ratio. Every person moving from frame one.
Continuity: Characters, props, environment identical across every cut. No identity drift.

CAMERA RIG (this scene): MAXIMUM VIOLENT handheld throughout in EVERY single shot — heavy aggressive jitter, breathing rig, reactive corrections, frame nearly destabilized at all times, the most extreme shake of the entire scene. Operator stationary in each shot — no walking, no dollying, no tracking, no arcing. Allowed within a shot: rotational pan/tilt to follow action, lens-driven zoom, rack focus. Even in close-ups the frame trembles intensely without exception.

Some thick smoke puffs out from the strike point. A handful of ROCK CHUNKS shear off the wall and tumble outward. Hairline cracks race from the crater. The blurred bell in soft foreground reverberates faintly from the shockwave, swinging on chain. The detonation is real and forceful but PROPORTIONAL to a small slingshot-fired charge — not a movie-set megablast. Camera SHAKES VIOLENTLY at peak intensity, jolting hard on the impact. Clean miss — moderate localized destruction.

SHOT 4 — LOW ANGLE MEDIUM CLOSE-UP OF EXPLODING WALL, ROCKS FALL PAST CAMERA (low angle medium close-up, MAXIMUM VIOLENT handheld).
Framing: LOW ANGLE medium close-up on the section of rugged cave-rock wall where the charge hit, camera planted at floor-level looking UP at the wall section. The wall fills the frame as a textured stone surface — weathered rock, fissured surface, with the fresh CRATER from the explosion visible in the upper portion of frame, smoke and embers still glowing in the impact point, hairline cracks radiating outward across the rock. Action: At the start of the shot, the wall is mid-collapse from the detonation — fresh smoke pouring out of the crater, glowing embers, cracks still spreading. Then ROCK CHUNKS begin to BREAK FREE from around the impact crater — fragments of stone of varying sizes sheared loose by the blast. They TUMBLE DOWNWARD with mass-correct gravity, falling from the upper portion of frame TOWARD the camera lens. Several large chunks fall PAST the camera between us and the wall — passing close to the lens, blurring as they cross the foreground in heavy motion blur, dust trailing behind them, smaller pebbles and fragments raining around them. We see the rocks as DARK SHAPES streaking down past the foreground of frame, their mass tangible. Some bounce off the wall lower down on their way down, some pass clean past the camera. Cold light from above catches the falling debris in stark relief. Behind/above the falling rocks the wall continues to crumble, more cracks racing outward. Camera SHAKES VIOLENTLY throughout — maximum handheld jitter, the operator flinching back from the falling stone close to the lens, frame nearly destabilized from the chaos. We are dangerously close to the collapse, debris narrowly missing the lens.
