## 原文 (source)

Edit this winter landscape image. Move the camera position to the 
side so we view the same exact scene from a different angle. The 
camera shifts left or right by a moderate amount, creating a 
diagonal three-quarter perspective view of the location instead of 
the current straight-on frontal view.

DESCRIBE THE NEW CAMERA ANGLE VISUALLY:
The camera is no longer pointing straight at the tree from the 
front. Instead the camera has moved sideways approximately 35-45 
degrees around the tree, and now points at the tree from the side-
front diagonal. As a result:

   • The tree is no longer perfectly frontal — we now see it from 
     a diagonal angle showing its front and one side simultaneously
   • The branches that were flat profile from the frontal view now 
     reveal depth and thickness from the new angle
   • One side of the tree trunk is visible alongside the front, 
     showing the trunk's three-dimensional cylindrical shape
   • The exposed roots at the base are now seen from a side-front 
     angle, showing their gnarled three-dimensional structure
   • The wind-shaped canopy is now angled in the frame, the curved 
     branch sweep visible from a slightly side perspective
   • Foreground rocks have shifted in their relative position to 
     the tree due to camera movement (parallax) — the rocks closer 
     to camera shift more than rocks farther away
   • The horizon line and background atmosphere reposition slightly 
     in the frame to reflect the new camera position
   • Shadows fall slightly differently as the relationship between 
     light direction and camera changes

The composition is no longer symmetric or frontal — it's a 
diagonal cinematic angle, like a documentary cinematographer 
walking 30-40 steps to one side of the original camera position 
and re-framing the same scene. The tree remains roughly central in 
the frame but now angled.

PRESERVE THE EXACT LOCATION AND ALL ELEMENTS:

Every physical element of the scene stays identical — same exact 
tree (same bark, same branches, same dried red leaves, same root 
structure), same rock formations, same dried red bushes, same pale 
ornamental grasses, same snow drift patterns, same vegetation 
details, same overall environment.

The atmospheric conditions stay identical — same dense fog and 
haze, same overcast grey-blue sky, same falling snow particles, 
same diffuse cold winter light quality, same color grade with cool 
blue-grey tones and dried red accents, same contrast and exposure 
levels, same cinematic mood.

The photographic quality stays identical — same film grain texture, 
same anamorphic film still aesthetic, same cinematic widescreen 
character.

ONLY THE CAMERA ANGLE CHANGES. Everything else preserved.

DO NOT:
— Do NOT change the tree itself (shape, branches, leaves, roots, 
  bark) — just show it from the new angle
— Do NOT replace the tree with a different tree
— Do NOT change the type or arrangement of vegetation
— Do NOT change the rocks or their character
— Do NOT change the snow appearance or texture
— Do NOT change the sky or atmospheric mood
— Do NOT change the lighting direction or quality
— Do NOT change the color grade or palette
— Do NOT change the film grain or photographic quality
— Do NOT add or remove any objects from the scene
— Do NOT pull phantom shapes from the fog or haze

PRESERVE EXACTLY:
— The tree identity completely
— All vegetation, rocks, terrain features
— Snow accumulation patterns
— Atmospheric haze and fog
— Sky and clouds
— Lighting and color
— Film grain and photographic texture
— Aspect ratio
— Cinematic mood

The result: same exact winter plateau location with the same exact 
tree and elements, just viewed from a diagonal three-quarter side 
angle instead of straight-on frontal. Imagine you walked 35 steps 
to one side and took a new photo of the same scene from there — 
that's the result we want.

Photorealistic, real practical location photography, ARRI Alexa 
anamorphic film still aesthetic. Match the texture, grain, color 
grade, and lighting quality of the input image exactly. Same 
location, diagonal three-quarter side angle perspective, every 
element preserved. Not CGI, not stylized, not a different tree, 
not a different location, not a regeneration from scratch, just a 
camera move to the side viewing the same scene.

## 中文对照 (zh)

编辑这张冬日风景图。把机位移到一侧，使我们从不同角度观看同一完全相同的场景。镜头向左或向右移动中等距离，营造出该地点的斜向四分之三透视视角，而非当前笔直的正面视角。

视觉上描述新机位：
镜头不再从正面正对树。相反，镜头已横向移动约 35-45 度绕树而转，如今从侧前方斜向对着树。结果：

   • 树不再完全正对正面——如今我们从斜向角度观看它，同时显示其正面与一个侧面
   • 正面视角下呈平面外形的枝条，如今从新角度显现出深浅与粗细
   • 树干的一个侧面与正面同时可见，显现出树干的三维圆柱形
   • 基部裸露的根系如今从侧前方角度观看，显现出盘结的三维结构
   • 迎风树冠如今在画面中倾斜，弯弧的枝条扫掠从略侧的角度可见
   • 前景岩石相对树的位置因镜头移动而改变（视差）——离镜头近的岩石比远的岩石移动得更明显
   • 地平线与背景氛围在画面中略微重排，以反映新的机位
   • 随着光线方向与镜头关系的变化，阴影的落向略有不同

构图不再是对称或正面的——它是一个斜向的电影角度，好比一位纪实摄影师从原机位向一侧走了 30-40 步，重新框住同一场景。树在画面中大致保持居中，但如今是倾斜的。

大气条件保持相同——相同的浓雾与雾霭、相同的灰蓝阴天天空、相同的落雪颗粒、相同的漫射冷冬光质感、相同的冷蓝灰调与干红点缀的色调、相同的反差与曝光级数、相同的电影氛围。

照片质感保持相同——相同的胶片颗粒纹理、相同的变形电影剧照美学、相同的电影宽银幕性格。

只有机位改变。其他一切保全。

不要：
— 不要改变树本身（形状、枝条、叶片、根、树皮）——只从新角度呈现它
— 不要用另一棵树替换这棵树
— 不要改变植被的类型或布置

严格保留：
— 树的身份完全保全
— 所有植被、岩石、地形特征
— 积雪堆积图案
— 大气雾霭与薄雾
