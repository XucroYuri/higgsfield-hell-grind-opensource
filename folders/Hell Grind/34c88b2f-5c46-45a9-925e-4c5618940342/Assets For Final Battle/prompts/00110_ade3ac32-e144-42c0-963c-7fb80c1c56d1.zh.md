## 原文 (source)

Edit this winter landscape image. Change ONLY the open sky areas 
to a dramatic stormy overcast sky. Do not change anything else in 
the image. Do not change the sky visible through the tree branches 
and canopy area — that sky remains exactly as it currently is.

SCOPE OF EDIT — SKY ONLY IN OPEN AREAS:
The only change in this edit is the sky in open areas of the frame 
where the sky is fully visible — meaning the sky to the left of the 
tree, the sky to the right of the tree, and the sky above the tree 
canopy in any portion of the frame where the open sky meets the 
horizon or fills the upper frame without the tree in front of it.

REPLACE THE OPEN SKY WITH DRAMATIC STORMY OVERCAST:
The new sky in the open areas is heavy stormy overcast — dense 
brooding storm clouds in deep grey and slate blue tones, dark moody 
cloud masses with subtle texture and turbulent cloud movement, 
heavier and darker than the current calm overcast, oppressive 
atmospheric weight, dramatic post-storm or pre-storm mood. The 
clouds have visible structure and depth — not flat uniform grey, 
but layered cloud masses with darker areas and lighter break 
patches, the kind of stormy sky a Roger Deakins or Roger Edens 
cinematography frame would have. Deep grey-blue palette, no warm 
tones in the sky, cold dramatic atmosphere.

CRITICAL — SKY THROUGH TREE BRANCHES STAYS UNCHANGED:
The sky visible through the tree branches, between the leaves, 
through gaps in the canopy, and behind the tree's silhouette must 
remain EXACTLY as it currently is in the input image. Do NOT 
change the sky color, sky tone, or sky texture in any pixel where 
the sky is seen through or behind the tree's branches. The tree 
silhouette and the sky reading through its branches stays 
unchanged.

This means the dramatic stormy sky applies only to the large open 
sky zones of the frame, while the sky pixels that are visible 
through the tree's gaps, between branches, and behind the tree 
stay identical to the input.

PRESERVE EVERYTHING ELSE EXACTLY:
— The tree completely (shape, branches, leaves, bark, roots)
— The sky pixels behind and through the tree's branches
— Foreground snow, vegetation, rocks, dried red bushes, ornamental 
  grasses
— Middle distance and background terrain
— Atmospheric haze and fog at ground level
— Falling snow particles
— Lighting direction and quality on the landscape (not changed by 
  the new sky)
— Color grade and palette of the foreground and tree
— Film grain and photographic quality
— Composition, framing, aspect ratio
— Camera angle exactly as input

DO NOT:
— Do NOT change the sky pixels visible through or behind the tree
— Do NOT change the tree or any of its details
— Do NOT change the foreground or vegetation
— Do NOT change the lighting on the landscape
— Do NOT change the color grade of non-sky areas
— Do NOT change the atmospheric ground-level fog and haze
— Do NOT add new elements
— Do NOT pull phantom shapes from the fog or clouds

The result: same exact winter landscape with the same exact tree 
and foreground and atmosphere, only the open sky areas (left of 
tree, right of tree, large open sky zones) are changed to a 
dramatic stormy overcast sky with deep grey-blue brooding cloud 
masses, while the sky reading through and behind the tree's 
branches remains unchanged.

Photorealistic, real practical photography quality, match the 
texture, grain, and base aesthetic of the input image. Not CGI, 
not stylized, only the open sky areas changed to dramatic stormy 
overcast, sky behind tree preserved exactly.
