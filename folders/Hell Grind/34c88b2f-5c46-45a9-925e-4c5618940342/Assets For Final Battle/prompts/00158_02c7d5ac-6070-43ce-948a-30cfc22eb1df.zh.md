Edit this winter landscape image. Add post-battle aftermath fire 
and smoke in the middle distance of the frame, matching the 
reference screenshot where fires burn on the ground at distance 
from camera. Preserve everything else exactly as it is in the 
input image.

CRITICAL ATMOSPHERIC PRESERVATION:
The thick atmospheric haze and fog density of the input image must 
remain EXACTLY as it is. The fog stays dense, the visibility limit 
stays the same, do NOT enhance fog details, do NOT pull additional 
shapes or forms out of the fog, do NOT add tree silhouettes or 
hidden objects in the haze, do NOT introduce phantom shapes or 
artifacts into the foggy areas, do NOT generate any new content in 
the atmospheric haze zones beyond what is explicitly described 
below. Treat the dense fog as a fixed atmospheric layer to preserve 
intact.

ADD FIRE PATCHES AT DIFFERENT DEPTHS (not in a horizontal row):
Three fire patches placed at distinctly DIFFERENT distances from 
camera, scattered at varying depths in the landscape, NOT aligned 
in a horizontal row, NOT at the same distance from viewer.

   • FIRE 1 — closest of the three fires, in the closer middle 
     distance NOT foreground, larger and more prominent flames, 
     positioned on the right side of the frame
   • FIRE 2 — at a medium distance from camera, positioned on the 
     left side of the frame closer to camera than fire 3 but 
     farther than fire 1, medium size flames
   • FIRE 3 — farthest of the three fires, way back near the 
     horizon line through the haze, smaller flames barely visible 
     through the atmospheric distance, positioned in the center-
     left of the frame, more atmospheric and distant

The depth variation creates natural battlefield scatter — the 
fires are at three distinct distance planes, each one further 
back than the next, fading into the haze with distance. NOT a row 
across the frame, NOT a flat horizontal arrangement.

The fire patches show visible orange-red flames burning on the 
snow surface, sized and brightness-varied by distance — closer fire 
brighter and larger, distant fire smaller and softer through haze. 
The snow around stays clean white. NO dirt, NO mud, NO scorched 
ground.

ADD SMOKE COLUMNS only from those specific fire patches with 
distance-appropriate scale — closer fire has thicker more visible 
smoke column, middle fire has medium smoke, distant fire smoke 
fades faster into the haze. Smoke columns NOT spreading broadly 
into the existing fog haze, just specific columns at specific 
locations.

ADJUST THE SKY ONLY to slightly darker stormy quality — heavier 
darker overcast cloud cover with deep grey-purple cloud masses, 
subtle warm orange undertones in the sky where it meets the 
horizon reflecting the distant fires. Do NOT change the fog at 
ground level.

PRESERVE EXACTLY:
— The existing dense atmospheric fog and haze, untouched
— All existing foreground elements: snow, rocks, vegetation, dried 
  red bushes and grass
— Clean white snow throughout
— Same exact composition and framing
— Same exact aspect ratio
— Same falling snow particles
— Same exact film grain and photographic quality
— No fire in foreground close to camera
— No dirt or mud anywhere
— No new shapes pulled from the fog
— No phantom trees or objects in haze
— Same exact texture, lighting, and grain as input image

Photorealistic, real practical location photography, ARRI Alexa 
anamorphic film still aesthetic, three fire patches at varying 
depths creating natural scatter NOT a horizontal row, otherwise 
the image exactly as input. Match the texture, grain, and lighting 
quality of the input image. Not CGI, not stylized, not phantom 
shapes in fog, not horizontal row of fires, not even distribution, 
varied depth scatter only.
